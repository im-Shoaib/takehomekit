How to quote a freelance project

A good quote is built from the work, not from a number that “feels right”. Break the project into tasks, estimate hours for each, and multiply by your rate. Then adjust for the things that always happen:

  • Revision buffer. Clients ask for changes. A 10–20% buffer pays for them without arguing over every round.
  • Rush fee. If the deadline means late nights or moving other clients, charge for it — 25–50% is common.
  • Platform fee. On Fiverr or Upwork, gross up the quote so the fee comes out of the client's price, not your pocket.
  • Deposit. Asking for 30–50% upfront on direct clients protects you if a project stalls.

Quote = (Σ hours × rate) × (1 + buffer) × (1 + rush) × (1 − discount) ÷ (1 − platform fee)

Frequently asked questions

How much buffer should I add to a freelance quote?

10–20% covers normal revisions and small surprises. Use more for clients who haven't given a clear brief.

Should I show the client the hourly breakdown?

Usually no. Clients compare outcomes, not hours. Use the breakdown to set your price, and present the total with a clear scope.

How do I include Fiverr or Upwork fees in my quote?

Tick “Add the platform fee to the quote”. The calculator divides by (1 − fee) so that after the platform takes its share, you keep your full amount.

Is my quote saved?

Yes, in your browser only. Nothing is sent to a server. Use “Start over” to clear it.
