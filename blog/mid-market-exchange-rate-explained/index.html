The mid-market rate: why you get less than Google's rate

You search “USD to PKR”, see one rate, then withdraw and get fewer rupees. The gap isn't an error — it's a fee hidden inside the rate. Here's how to measure it.

Key points
  • The mid-market rate is the midpoint between buy and sell prices — the rate you see on Google or XE.
  • Most providers pay you a lower rate and keep the difference. That's a fee, even if it's not labelled one.
  • Measure it: (mid-market − your rate) ÷ mid-market × 100 gives the hidden fee in %.
  • Compare providers on final rupees received, not on “zero fees” claims.

What the mid-market rate is

In currency markets, there are always two prices: what buyers will pay and what sellers will accept. The mid-market rate is the point exactly between them. It's the rate you see on Google, XE and most currency converters, and it's the fairest reference for “what my dollars are worth right now”.

Individuals almost never get it directly. Banks, Payoneer and money apps buy at one price and pay you another, keeping the difference — called the spread or markup.

Why your rate is lower

  • The conversion markup. A provider might pay you a rate a percent or two below mid-market. On Payoneer withdrawals to PKR, a markup of around 2% is typical; the exact rate is shown before you confirm.
  • Timing. Rates move all day. Google updates frequently; your provider may refresh on its own schedule.
  • Bank-side rates. If dollars arrive in a PKR account, your bank converts at its own buying rate.

How to work out the hidden fee

Hidden fee % = (mid-market rate − rate you got) ÷ mid-market rate × 100

Example (illustrative numbers): mid-market is Rs 280 per dollar and you receive Rs 274.40. The difference is Rs 5.60, so the hidden fee is 5.60 ÷ 280 × 100 = 2%. On a $500 withdrawal, that's Rs 2,800 — before any visible fee.

The total cost of a withdrawal

Add up everything between “money in my account” and “rupees in my bank”:

  1. Any receiving fee (for example, when a client pays you directly into Payoneer).
  2. Any flat withdrawal fee (Fiverr charges $3 per withdrawal to Payoneer).
  3. The conversion markup hidden in the rate.

Then compare what you actually received with “amount × mid-market rate”. That gap is your true total cost.

Ways to lose less

  • Withdraw less often, in bigger amounts, so flat fees are spread across more money. The percentage markup still applies to the whole amount.
  • Compare routes using the final rupee amount, not advertised fees.
  • Check the rate before confirming. Payoneer shows the rate and amount on the confirmation screen.
  • Keep dollars where it makes sense. If you pay for software or services in dollars, paying from your USD balance avoids converting twice.

“Zero fee” offers

When a service says “no fees”, check the rate. A transfer with no visible fee but a 3% worse rate costs more than one with a small fee and a near-mid-market rate. Final amount received is the only fair comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What is the mid-market exchange rate?

The midpoint between the buying and selling price of a currency — the rate shown on Google and XE.

Why does Payoneer give me less than Google's rate?

Payoneer, like most providers, applies a conversion markup to the rate. The difference between its rate and mid-market is effectively a fee.

How do I calculate the hidden exchange rate fee?

Subtract the rate you got from the mid-market rate, divide by the mid-market rate and multiply by 100.

Can I get the mid-market rate?

Some providers convert at or very close to it and charge a visible fee instead. Compare the final amount you receive.
