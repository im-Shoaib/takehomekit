How to raise your freelance rates without losing clients

If you're always busy, your prices are telling you something. Here's how to raise them with confidence — and keep the clients you want.

Key points
  • Signs it's time: you're fully booked, clients accept quotes instantly, or your costs and fees have gone up.
  • Raise in steps of 10–20%, starting with new clients.
  • Give existing clients notice — 30 days is common — and a clear date.
  • Re-check your minimum rate first, so the new price covers fees, tax and time.

Signs your rates are too low

  • You're always fully booked and turning work away.
  • Clients say yes immediately — no questions, no negotiation.
  • Your costs have risen: software, internet, platform fees, inflation.
  • Your skills have grown — better results, faster delivery, a stronger portfolio.
  • You haven't raised prices in a year or more.

Step 1: Recalculate your floor

Before deciding the new price, work out the minimum your work must earn. Include your income goal, business costs, tax and platform fees, and be honest about billable hours.

Step 2: Decide how much to raise

For most freelancers, 10–20% at a time is a comfortable step: noticeable for your income, small enough that good clients rarely leave. If you're far below your floor, raise in two steps a few months apart rather than one big jump.

On Fiverr or Upwork, remember fees are taken from the new price too. To keep $50 more per order on Fiverr, the list price must rise by $62.50.

Step 3: Start with new clients

New clients only ever see your new price, so there's nothing to explain. Update your gig packages, rate card and quotes first. If new clients accept the higher price, you've confirmed the market supports it.

Step 4: Tell existing clients — kindly and clearly

Good clients expect prices to rise over time. What they dislike is surprise. Give notice, a date and a reason in one or two sentences.

Hi [name],

I've really enjoyed working with you on [project / ongoing work]. I'm writing to let you know that from [date, about 30 days away] my rate will be [new rate], up from [old rate].

Any work booked before that date stays at the current rate. Thank you for your continued trust — I'm looking forward to the next project.

Best,
[your name]
  • Don't over-apologise or justify at length.
  • Offer a grace period for work already planned.
  • For your best long-term clients, you might keep their rate a little longer — that's your choice, not an obligation.

Raising prices on Fiverr

  • Change one package at a time and watch orders for a couple of weeks.
  • Add value as you raise: faster delivery, an extra revision, source files.
  • Use your strongest reviews and portfolio in the gig to justify the new price.

Raising prices on Upwork

  • Update your profile rate for new proposals.
  • For ongoing hourly contracts, message the client with notice before changing the rate.
  • For fixed-price work, simply quote the new price for the next milestone or project.

What if a client leaves?

Some will — usually the most price-sensitive ones, who are often the hardest to work with. Losing one low-paying client frees time for better-paying work. If you're worried, raise rates for new clients first and existing clients a few months later.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I raise my freelance rates?

10–20% at a time is a common, comfortable step. If you're well below your minimum rate, raise in two steps a few months apart.

How much notice should I give clients?

About 30 days is common. Include the date and keep work already booked at the old rate.

Will I lose clients if I raise my rates?

You may lose a few price-sensitive clients, but most good clients expect gradual increases, and the time you free up can go to better-paying work.

Fees, limits and rules change. We check them regularly and date every update, but always confirm the final number with the platform, your provider or an adviser before you rely on it.
