How to file your tax return as a freelancer in Pakistan

Filing once a year is what unlocks the low final tax on your foreign earnings and keeps you on the Active Taxpayers List. Here's the whole process, start to finish.

Key points
  • Filing your return is what keeps you on the Active Taxpayers List (ATL) — and the reduced 0.25% / 1% final tax on IT exports depends on it.
  • The tax year runs 1 July to 30 June. Returns are normally due by 30 September.
  • You file two things on FBR's IRIS portal: the return of income and the wealth statement.
  • Filing late now costs more: from tax year 2026, joining the ATL late carries a Rs 25,000 surcharge.

Why filing matters for freelancers

Many freelancers assume that because their bank already deducts tax on foreign remittances, there's nothing else to do. That's the most expensive misunderstanding in Pakistani freelancing.

  • The low rate depends on it. Export earnings from IT and IT-enabled services can be taxed under the final tax regime — 0.25% with PSEB registration, 1% without — but only if you file your return and meet the other conditions. Our PSEB guide explains the rates.
  • Non-filers pay more everywhere. People not on the ATL face higher withholding tax on many transactions, from bank withdrawals to property and vehicles.
  • Your income becomes provable. A filed return, bank statements and PRCs together show where your money came from — useful for visas, loans and large purchases.

Before you start: what to gather

  • CNIC, and a mobile number and email registered in your own name (IRIS sends codes to both).
  • Bank statements for the full tax year, for every account.
  • Payoneer (or other provider) statements showing what you received and withdrew.
  • PRCs — the bank's Proceeds Realisation Certificates for your foreign remittances.
  • Tax deduction certificates — the tax your bank withheld on remittances or other transactions.
  • Invoices or platform earnings reports that match your remittances.
  • Details of assets and liabilities on 30 June: bank balances, cash, property, vehicles, loans — needed for the wealth statement.

Step 1: Register on IRIS (first time only)

If you've never filed, register as a taxpayer on FBR's IRIS portal with your CNIC. Your CNIC number becomes your NTN as an individual. You'll verify your mobile number and email with one-time codes and set a password.

Step 2: Open the return for the right tax year

Log in, start a new return of income for the individual and choose the correct tax year — tax year 2026 for income earned from July 2025 to June 2026. IRIS menus change between versions, so look for the option to file the annual return for individuals.

Step 3: Declare your freelance income

  • Declare your foreign freelance earnings as export of IT / IT-enabled services, so they fall under the final tax regime rather than normal slab rates.
  • Use the PKR amounts your bank credited, which match your PRCs.
  • Declare any other income separately — a salary, local clients, profit on savings.

Step 4: Enter the tax already deducted

Add the tax your bank withheld on your remittances (and any other withholding taxes) in the tax deductions or adjustable / final tax section. Your bank's tax certificate shows these amounts. If the tax already deducted equals the final tax on that income, there's usually nothing extra to pay on it.

Step 5: Complete the wealth statement

Every individual who files must also submit a wealth statement: your assets and liabilities on 30 June, and a reconciliation showing how your wealth changed during the year (income in, personal expenses out). Your net assets at the end should be explained by last year's figure plus your income minus your spending.

Take care here. Unexplained increases in wealth are the most common reason for questions from FBR.

Step 6: Submit and keep the acknowledgement

Review everything, submit both the return and the wealth statement, and save the acknowledgement receipt. Your name should appear on the ATL once FBR updates the list.

What happens if you file late

From tax year 2026, an individual who files after the deadline and wants to be on the ATL faces a Rs 25,000 surcharge — up from Rs 1,000 before. In October 2026 FBR published a draft rule that would let late filers join the ATL without the surcharge by signing an undertaking not to buy property for six months; at the time of writing it was a proposal, not law. Either way, filing on time is far cheaper.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Declaring export income as ordinary business income — it can push you onto slab rates.
  • Using dollar figures instead of the PKR amounts your bank actually credited.
  • Skipping the wealth statement or leaving the reconciliation unbalanced.
  • Losing PRCs — request them as you go, not in September.
  • Waiting until the last week — IRIS is slowest just before the deadline.

If your situation is more complex — several income sources, property, a registered business — a tax practitioner is worth the fee.

Frequently asked questions

When is the deadline to file a tax return in Pakistan?

Normally 30 September after the tax year ends (the tax year runs July to June). The government sometimes extends it, but you shouldn't rely on that.

Do freelancers need to file a return if the bank already deducted tax?

Yes. Filing keeps you on the Active Taxpayers List, and the reduced final tax on IT exports depends on it.

What is the penalty for filing late?

From tax year 2026, joining the ATL after filing late carries a Rs 25,000 surcharge. FBR has proposed an alternative undertaking route, but it wasn't in force at the time of writing.

Do I need to file a wealth statement?

Yes. Individuals filing a return also submit a wealth statement listing assets and liabilities on 30 June, with a reconciliation of how wealth changed during the year.

Fees, limits and rules change. We check them regularly and date every update, but always confirm the final number with the platform, your provider or an adviser before you rely on it.
