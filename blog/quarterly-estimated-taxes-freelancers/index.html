Quarterly estimated taxes for US freelancers

No employer withholds tax from freelance income, so the IRS expects you to pay during the year. Here's who has to, when, and how much.

Key points
  • You generally must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year after withholding and credits.
  • 2026 tax year due dates: 15 April, 15 June, 15 September 2026 and 15 January 2027.
  • Payments cover income tax and self-employment tax.
  • Safe harbor: pay 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if your AGI was over $150,000) to avoid the underpayment penalty.

Who needs to pay estimated taxes

According to the IRS, individuals — including sole proprietors and freelancers — generally need to make estimated tax payments if they expect to owe $1,000 or more when they file their return. If you have a day job with withholding, you can sometimes increase withholding instead.

2026 due dates

Income earnedPayment due
1 January – 31 March 202615 April 2026
1 April – 31 May 202615 June 2026
1 June – 31 August 202615 September 2026
1 September – 31 December 202615 January 2027

The “quarters” aren't equal. If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day.

What the payments cover

  • Self-employment tax — Social Security and Medicare, 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings (the Social Security part stops at the annual wage base).
  • Federal income tax on your profit, after the deduction for half of self-employment tax and your standard or itemised deduction.

How to calculate each payment

  1. Estimate your net profit for the year (income minus business expenses).
  2. Estimate the total tax on it — self-employment tax plus income tax.
  3. Subtract any withholding and credits you expect.
  4. Divide by four for each payment, or use the safe harbor amount below.

The IRS Form 1040-ES includes a worksheet for this. If your income is uneven — a quiet first half and a busy second half — the annualised income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) can lower earlier payments.

Safe harbor: how to avoid the penalty

You generally won't owe an underpayment penalty if your total payments on time are at least the smaller of:

  • 90% of the tax for the current year, or
  • 100% of the tax on last year's return (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was more than $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).

The prior-year rule is the simplest for many freelancers: take last year's total tax, divide by four, and pay that each quarter. You settle any difference when you file.

How to pay

  • IRS Direct Pay — free, from a bank account.
  • Your IRS Online Account.
  • EFTPS — the Treasury's free payment system.
  • Debit or credit card through approved processors (they charge a fee).
  • Check or money order with a Form 1040-ES voucher.

Most states with an income tax have their own estimated payment system — check your state's revenue department.

Practical tips

  • Set aside a percentage of every payment in a separate account so the money is there on the due date.
  • Put the four dates in your calendar with a reminder a week before.
  • Price for tax. Your rate needs to cover it — our pricing guide shows how.

Frequently asked questions

When are quarterly estimated taxes due for 2026?

15 April 2026, 15 June 2026, 15 September 2026 and 15 January 2027. Dates on weekends or holidays move to the next business day.

Do I have to pay estimated taxes as a freelancer?

Generally yes if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year after withholding and credits.

How do I avoid the estimated tax penalty?

Pay on time at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000).

How do I pay estimated taxes online?

Use IRS Direct Pay, your IRS Online Account or EFTPS, all free from a bank account.
