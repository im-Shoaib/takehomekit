Why a simple contract matters

Most freelance disputes come from three things: unclear scope, endless revisions and late payment. A one-page agreement that both sides read before work starts prevents almost all of them. It doesn't need legal jargon — it needs to be specific.

What this agreement covers

  1. Parties and project — who is working with whom, on what.
  2. Scope and deliverables — exactly what's included, and that anything else is extra.
  3. Timeline — start and delivery dates, and what happens if feedback is late.
  4. Fee and payment — the total and when each part is due.
  5. Revisions and extra work — how many rounds are included and the rate beyond that.
  6. Ownership — the client owns the final work once they've paid in full.
  7. Confidentiality, cancellation, liability and governing law.

Tips for using it

  • Be specific in the deliverables. “Five-page website” is better than “website”. “Two logo concepts, three revision rounds” is better than “logo design”.
  • Get agreement in writing. A signed PDF is best, but a client replying “I agree” by email is far better than nothing.
  • On Fiverr and Upwork, the platform's terms govern the order. Use this to put the scope in writing and attach it to the order or contract.

Frequently asked questions

Is this contract legally binding?

A written agreement both parties accept is generally enforceable, but laws vary by country. This template is a plain-language starting point, not legal advice — have a lawyer review it for large or high-risk projects.

Do I need a contract for Fiverr or Upwork orders?

The platform's terms apply to marketplace orders, but a written scope still prevents disputes. For direct clients, a contract is essential.

Can I edit the generated text?

Yes. Copy the text into Word or Google Docs and change anything you need, or adjust the fields here and download a new PDF.
